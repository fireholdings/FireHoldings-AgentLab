## A. Executive investment decision

**Launch one business: a contractor-compliance service.** It gets small subcontractors approved, and keeps them approved, on the prequalification platforms their customers require (ISNetworld, Avetta, Veriforce), by writing the required safety programs, fixing rejected documents and maintaining the account.

Why it wins, on the evidence gathered:

- **Forced purchase.** A subcontractor cannot work on the hiring client's site until approved. New hiring clients joined ISNetworld through 2026 (Vitro, Potentia, DESRI, IPX Power — titles seen, pages unread).
- **One sale is roughly $1,000.** A competitor sells an ISNetworld manual at $1,495 one-time or $95/month; another charges from $300/month; a full-service firm lists $7,950/year. Every other finalist needs 3–7 sales or has a legal problem.
- **Large, stable base.** Company-stated counts: ISN about 90,000 contractors, Avetta about 130,000, Veriforce 140,000+.
- **Buyers exist for the category.** EQT bought Avetta (reported about $3B, 2024); KPA, Veriforce and Avetta have made add-on acquisitions. No small-deal price was found.
- **Founder fit.** Writing and maintaining audit-ready programs in regulated environments is what the founder has done. This helps delivery; no evidence shows it wins the sale.

**Two honest qualifiers.**

1. **No opportunity researched is credibly likely to collect $1,000 in 30 days from cold outreach.** The red team reached that verdict for this business and for trucking. The 30 days should be run as a sales test with fixed abandon rules (section K), using the founder's own network first, because every documented fast-revenue case found was sold to existing contacts or by direct personal outreach.
2. **The base-case model does not reach $5M.** It reaches roughly $2–2.7M enterprise value in year 5. $5M needs the aggressive case: about 1,200 subscribing clients and a management team (section H).

**One business or two?** One. A separate cash business would split a solo founder's selling time, and none of the candidates is clearly faster. Paid security or training work for people the founder already knows is acceptable as bridge income, under the same brand, when it comes to him.

**On Mudcat.** No description of the Mudcat concept was supplied, so it could not be tested. As a name it suits a blue-collar industrial audience better than "Rambunctious Rascals", which is a liability on a compliance invoice. Use a trade name; do not spend on branding before the first sale.

## C. Top 5 deep dives

**1. Contractor prequalification service (recommended).**
- *Offer:* fixed-fee approval package, then monthly maintenance (quarterly updates, insurance certificates, training records).
- *For:* large addressable base; hard site-access deadline; recurring by nature because requirements change and renew.
- *Against:* dozens of incumbents, one of which invoices only after ISN accepts the manual; Avetta sells its own managed service and AI document review; no safety credential; liability if a program is wrong (insurance about $2,200/year, above the launch budget).
- *Platform terms:* ISN's user agreement limits users to the subscriber's employees or independent contractors and endorses no consultants. Read the full agreement; work only as a named user the client adds, never on shared logins. Veriforce and Avetta terms were not seen.

**2. Trucking new-entrant readiness.**
- *For:* about 55,000 new USDOT numbers in the first half of 2025; public registrant data; active acquirers (Tenstreet, Fleetworthy, PrePass).
- *Against:* FMCSA itself warns new registrants about vendors who scrape that data; forum posts describe 20+ solicitation calls a day; FMCSA reports a 91.4% audit pass rate, so the fear pitch is weak; tickets of $30–$325; calls to mixed-use cell phones carry TCPA exposure. Rejected as the lead business.

**3. First-party receivables follow-up.**
- *For:* urgent, measurable pain; flat fee collects fast. The red team rated it the most likely of its three to reach $1,000 (its own estimate, 35–45%).
- *Against:* the licensing exemption is not a safe harbor. Wyoming's statute arguably covers a hired contractor and Michigan case law reads "collection agency" broadly. Needs a lawyer's opinion before any sale. Software does the reminders for $19–$259/month. No comparable sale found.

**4. Government bid/no-bid reviews.**
- *For:* a live solicitation deadline forces a decision; delivery is AI-assisted document work on public data.
- *Against:* APEX Accelerators give free help; small-business contractor count fell 4% in fiscal 2025; AI proposal tools list from $39–$60/month; lumpy revenue; weak exit.

**5. CMMC Level 1 packs.**
- *For:* self-assessment and annual affirmation remain in force; attested scores carry False Claims Act risk (a $4.6M settlement was cited).
- *Against:* Phase 2 was suspended on 13 July 2026 and no restart date was found; free templates and a $2,995 competitor exist; controlled data cannot go through a consumer AI plan; sales cycles of weeks to months.

## D. Immediate revenue winner

**Contractor prequalification, sold first to the founder's own network, is the best available bet — not a likely one.**

- It needs the fewest sales (one or two).

## E. First 72 hours

1. Read the full ISNetworld user agreement and Avetta and Veriforce supplier terms. If third-party help as a named user is prohibited, stop and reassess.
2. List 40 warm contacts: veterans who own or run contracting firms, Ross alumni in construction, energy and industrial services, former NNSA and DOE contractor colleagues.
3. Build one sample program set (hazard communication, lockout/tagout, fall protection) to the published ISN review protocol format. This is the demonstration.
4. Write a one-page engagement letter: scope, client-supplied inputs, no guarantee of approval, liability capped at fees paid. Have a lawyer glance at it when cash allows.
5. Request two professional-liability quotes. Do not buy yet.
6. Open a Stripe account and run one small test payment; the first payout takes about seven days.
7. Send the first 20 warm messages and place 10 calls.

Needs the founder's approval and action: every outreach message, any purchase, any account opening. None was done in this research.

## F. First 30-day sales plan

**Targets, in order.**
1. Warm network (40 contacts): ask who they know that has been told to join ISNetworld, Avetta or Veriforce.
2. Subcontractors of companies that recently mandated a platform (Vitro, Potentia, DESRI, IPX Power and any newer names). Find them through LinkedIn and state contractor-licence lists.
3. People who already hold the buyer: commercial insurance agents, safety-supply distributors, and existing consultancies that may subcontract overflow writing.

**Rules.** Personal email, LinkedIn and manual calls only. No texting, no autodialer, no purchased lists. Skip Florida, Oklahoma, Washington and Maryland numbers until advised. Never imply affiliation with a platform.

**Pricing.**
- Rejection fix: $350, paid in full upfront.
- Approval package: $1,250, half upfront, half on approval.
- Maintenance: $295/month, billed in advance, month to month.

**Warm message.** "I've started helping small contractors get approved on ISNetworld and Avetta — I write the safety programs and fix the rejections. Do you know anyone who's just been told by a customer they have to join? I'd take an introduction over a sale."

**Cold opener.** "I saw [client] now requires ISNetworld for its contractors. If your programs are getting kicked back or you haven't started, I do the writing and resubmissions for a fixed $1,250, half now and half when you're approved. Worth ten minutes?"

**Discovery questions.**
- Which customer is requiring it, and by what date?
- What is that customer worth to you a year?
- How many programs are required, and how many have been rejected?
- Who is doing this now, and what else is that person not doing?
- Have you been quoted elsewhere?

**Demonstration.** Screen-share the sample program set beside the reviewer checklist; then mark up one of the prospect's own rejected documents live.

**Proposal and payment.** One-page letter, Stripe invoice sent on the call, ACH preferred (0.8%, capped at $5; cards 2.9% + 30¢). Work starts when the deposit clears.

## I. Red-team report: ten reasons the recommendation fails

1. A competitor sells the manual for $1,495 and bills only after acceptance.
2. The founder has no safety credential, no reviews and no trade contacts.
3. Platform terms may restrict third-party submission; ISN can suspend accounts.
4. Avetta already sells managed compliance and AI review; platforms will keep absorbing the service layer.
5. Contractors can draft programs with general AI tools or a $497 wizard.
6. A negligently written program cited after an injury is an uninsured tail risk at launch.
7. Clients may cancel maintenance once approved.
8. Demand arrives in lumps tied to hiring-client deadlines; no live 2026 deadline was found.
9. The work is labor: at 200 clients it needs staff and quality control, and margins are unproven.
10. No sale of a small firm of this kind, at any price, was found.

## K. Founder decision brief

- **Launch:** a contractor-compliance service under a trade name.
- **Sell:** approval packages and monthly maintenance for ISNetworld, Avetta and Veriforce.
- **To:** owners and safety leads of 5–50 person subcontractors facing a hiring-client requirement.
- **Charge:** $350 rejection fix; $1,250 package (half upfront); $295/month maintenance.
- **First customer:** through 40 warm contacts and their introductions, then subcontractors of newly mandating companies.
- **First $1,000:** two deposits, or one completed package, invoiced by Stripe.
- **Spend:** under $300 before first revenue (domain, email, a short safety course). Insurance from first revenue.
- **Avoid:** trucking cold-calling, receivables work without a legal opinion, generic AI agency offers, any outcome guarantee, shared client logins.
- **Next seven days:** read platform terms, build the sample, open Stripe, reach all 40 contacts, hold ten conversations, send three proposals.
- **Pivot if, by day 21:**
  - 150+ targeted contacts produce fewer than three real conversations and no deposit;
  - a platform states in writing that third-party help breaches its terms;
  - prospects consistently choose the pay-after-pass manual;
  - no insurer will quote.

  Next test in that case: fleet driver-file audits for 5–50 driver carriers, sold by referral, not cold calls.
- **Path to $5M:** about 1,200 subscribing clients, $1M adjusted profit after a hired manager, sold to a compliance platform or roll-up at about 5×. The base case reaches about half of that.
