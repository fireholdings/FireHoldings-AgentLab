## 0. Key correction: CMMC Phase 2 is suspended, not live

- VERIFIED: On July 13, 2026 the Department of War suspended the CMMC Phase 2 transition, which was due Nov 10, 2026, and launched a 60-day Reform Task Force. Sources: Holland & Knight (hklaw.com, 2026-07-31), Troutman (2026-07-16), WilmerHale (2026-07-20), Crowell, GovConWire.
- VERIFIED (secureframe summary, Sept 2026): Program offices can designate only Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self). Level 2 (C3PAO) and Level 3 designations are not permitted, and no waivers will be granted. Existing C3PAO solicitations are being amended.
- VERIFIED: DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, and SPRS score submission remain in force. Jenner & Block's headline says enforcement risk remains, including False Claims Act exposure.
- VERIFIED: The task force report was due Sept 11, 2026 but was not public as of September coverage (insidegovernmentcontracts.com, Sept 2026). I found nothing newer.
- Some vendor blogs still say Phase 2 started Nov 10. Those are stale or wrong. Treat "C3PAO deadline panic" as weakened and uncertain.
- Net effect: demand for C3PAO-driven $100K programs has softened. Demand for cheap, defensible self-assessment and documentation work has not. SPRS scores are attested under the False Claims Act, so honest, documented scores are a legal-risk problem.

## 2. Deeper evidence on the top 3

### A. CMMC Level 1 self-assessment pack (#1)
- Demand is real because FAR 52.204-21 and CMMC Level 1 affirmation apply to every DoD supplier that handles FCI. Suspension only touches the Level 2 (C3PAO) track.
- VERIFIED: DoD's own estimate is $5,977/yr per small entity, mostly staff time. Vendors quote $5K–$15K for consultant-assisted work, and a packaged version at $5K–$8K.
- Risk: the $1,000 target is small relative to those prices, so a $1,500–$2,500 fixed-fee wedge is plausible. This is an ESTIMATE, not an observed conversion.
- Gap: owners fear False Claims Act exposure (VERIFIED, Jenner & Block alert and vendor blogs) and want defensible evidence.
- Missing evidence: how many small suppliers are actually being pressured by primes right now, and whether they buy a $2K service.

### B. NIST 800-171 SSP / POA&M for Level 2 (Self) (#2)
- Phase 2 suspension removes the C3PAO deadline, but Level 2 (Self) and 7012 still apply. Primes keep asking for SSPs and SPRS scores.
- Prices VERIFIED from vendor sources: gap assessment $3.5K–$30K; DoD models $34,277 for Level 2 self; $101,752 for third-party certification (now not required).
- The honest issue: many small firms can't implement the 110 controls. An SSP the client can't honestly attest to is the very thing that triggers the False Claims Act risk. A document-only offer must include a truthful POA&M.
- AI limit: CUI must not enter consumer AI tools. The offer should rely on client-answered questionnaires and local drafting.

### C. Fractional FSO support (#4)
- This is a trap if sold as outsourcing. VERIFIED (ClearanceJobs; 32 CFR 117): the FSO must be an employee. The offer must be "FSO support and training."
- Price points are scarce. Adamo says its rates scale by cleared-employee count and are unpublished; one provider says about $90/hr.
- Sales cycle: cleared small firms already know the problem, but trust is high-stakes. A recurring retainer is the long-term value, and the first sale could be a one-off self-inspection.
- This is the one area where the founder's NNSA security program management experience is a genuine trigger.

## 3. Honest assessment

**Real buying triggers from the founder's background (HYPOTHESIS, no evidence of buyers saying so):**
- FSO support, insider-threat programs, and DOE/NNSA flow-downs. Buyers in these segments value people who have actually run security programs in regulated environments.

**Nice to have only:**
- CMMC Level 1/2 documentation. Buyers care about price, speed, and defensibility, and Special Forces and NNSA background is a marketing line, not a purchase trigger. Competition from MSPs is intense.
- Workplace-violence training, executive protection, data-center plans. Military credibility helps marketing but does not make buyers urgent.

**Too long a sales cycle for $1,000 in 30 days (ESTIMATE):**
- NERC CIP-014 (utility procurement, unaffiliated-reviewer rules, effective date 2028).
- Prime-level supplier programs (#3).
- DOE/NNSA subcontract work (lab procurement and vendor onboarding).
- ITAR program build-outs (need legal-adjacent credibility).
- SDVOSB set-aside wins, which also depend on whether the founder has a VA disability rating (unknown).

**Plausible within 30 days:** a fixed-price Level 1 pack or a one-time SSP/POA&M review for a small supplier reached through existing network. Even this depends on whether the founder already knows 2–3 such suppliers. No conversion data was found; treat any outcome as unproven.

**Exit potential.** A solo consulting practice rarely sells for $5M. A $5M outcome would more likely come from a productized compliance platform or a recurring-revenue managed-compliance service, which changes the build cost. This is a HYPOTHESIS, not researched.

**Biggest uncertainties to verify next:** the task force's final outcome and whether Level 2 (C3PAO) returns; real small-supplier willingness to pay for Level 1; state licensing for security consulting (check Michigan LARA and Texas DPS directly); whether the founder qualifies for SDVOSB.
