## 3. What $5M EV requires (all ESTIMATE / arithmetic)

Owner-replacement adjustment: deduct a market-rate GM/CEO salary (assume $150-200K loaded) from owner profit before applying the multiple. A founder who draws $60K and shows $1.0M "profit" has about $0.85M of adjusted EBITDA, which is $4.3M at 5x.

**A. Recurring compliance service (5x EBITDA, range 4-6x)**
- Need $1.0M adjusted EBITDA (at 4x $1.25M; at 6x $0.83M).
- At 20-25% margin that is $4-5M revenue. At $120-150K revenue per employee (HYPOTHESIS; no compliance-specific source found), that is about 30-40 staff.
- With 60%+ recurring revenue, top-5 customers <30%, and a #2 manager, it can price at the upper end.

**B. Vertical SaaS (3-5x ARR, or about 3.9x SDE)**
- $5M EV needs about $1.0-1.7M ARR. On the SDE basis, it needs about $1.3M SDE.
- Net revenue retention above 100% and churn below ~10% annually (HYPOTHESIS) separate the 5x outcome from the 3x outcome.
- Buyers will adjust for founder support and developer time.

**C. Tech-enabled BPO (5.5x EBITDA)**
- Need about $0.9M adjusted EBITDA. At 10-15% margin that is $6-9M revenue.
- At $50-80K revenue per head (HYPOTHESIS), that is 100-150 staff. Labor-heavy and hard to bootstrap, and AI is compressing multiples.

**Illustrative net to founder on $5M EV** (HYPOTHESIS)
- ~70% cash at close, ~20% seller note over 2-3 years, ~10% earn-out (structure assumed; Pepperdine data not retrieved).
- Fees ~5-8%. Blended tax ~25% (federal capital gains 20% + 3.8% NIIT, plus state; ordinary-income recapture on asset deals).
- Net about $3.3-3.8M after tax and fees, of which ~$1.5M arrives later or is contingent. This leaves about 70% of the headline EV, so $5M EV is about $3.5M take-home.

## 4. Ranking by exit attractiveness (HYPOTHESIS, informed by the evidence above)

1. **Trucking/DOT and contractor-safety compliance software or managed services.** Active serial acquirers (Tenstreet, Fleetworthy, PrePass, KPA, Avetta/EQT), data and network lock-in, and recurring subscriptions. Small niche means few buyers, and buyers favor software over services.
2. **Compliance training (state-mandated or certification).** Clear PE rollup (360training) buying small online providers repeatedly. Recurring if tied to renewals and licensing cycles. Content is easy to copy and exits are small.
3. **Cyber/CMMC/managed GRC.** Strong strategic buyer interest (Thrive, Fortreum, BV/CyberSheath). Founder-dependent and credential-heavy, but managed (recurring) models earn premiums. Pure advisory is project revenue.
4. **Vertical SaaS under $2M ARR.** Highest ceiling and best multiples, but the weakest evidence on small private multiples, valuations have been falling, and building the product with $0-1K is the hardest route.
5. **RCM/AR outsourcing.** Real sponsor demand ($0.5-1.5M EBITDA platforms), but labor-intensive and exposed to AI automation and customer concentration.
6. **GovCon services.** Highest headline multiples in the data, but recompete risk, small-business set-aside eligibility loss at sale, DOGE disruption and long ramp time. Unrealistic from $0-1K.
7. **Tech-enabled BPO.** Lowest multiple (5.5x), thin margins, headcount-heavy.

## 5. Value killers and base rates

- **Founder dependence, customer concentration, project revenue and churn** are the standard discounts (advisor consensus; GovCon advisors say buyers value "what survives the transaction"). Typical response is earn-outs and seller notes, and under-$5M deals commonly include them (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Base rates.** I did not find any dataset on how often bootstrapped service firms reach $1M+ EBITDA in 5 years. The relevant facts found: 33% of startups survive five years (NBER/Census-linked paper), and employer establishments have a ~48.9% five-year survival rate (SBA citing Census, 1994-2020). A JPMorgan Chase Institute report found a small share of firms reach $1M revenue within 5 years. $1M EBITDA is a much higher bar, so I would estimate well under 1-3% of starts (HYPOTHESIS, unquantified).
- **Revenue per employee and margins for compliance-services firms**: no solid source found. The figures above are placeholders.
