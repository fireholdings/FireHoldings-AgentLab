# 08 - Healthcare / Insurance Admin / AR / Revenue Recovery (solo, unlicensed)

Date of research: 2026-11-11. About 13 searches, no page fetches. "VERIFIED" here means the claim appeared in a search-result summary of the named source. I did not open the underlying pages, and most price sources are vendor marketing, so treat prices as directional. ESTIMATE and HYPOTHESIS are mine. Gaps are flagged.

## Bottom line
- Fastest legal cash for an unlicensed solo: flat-fee or retainer services that are NOT contingency, NOT PHI-heavy, and NOT regulated adjusting. Top 3: (1) first-party AR follow-up done in the client's name, (2) credentialing / payer enrollment for small practices (flat fee), (3) tariff-refund (CAPE) filing prep and entry-reconciliation for small importers, sold flat-fee and routed through the importer of record or its broker.
- Contingency models (freight/telecom/merchant audit, ERC, sales tax, unclaimed property) usually take 60-180+ days to cash. They are unlikely to meet the $1,000-in-30-days goal unless sold as flat-fee audits.
- Exit logic: $5M+ needs recurring revenue and process IP. Contingency one-offs sell poorly. Recurring-retainer AR/credentialing/COI books are the most sellable, to RCM firms and BPOs.

## 2. Tariff refunds: current status
VERIFIED via search (not primary pages):
- Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs 6-3 on Feb 20, 2026. CBP estimated about $166B across 53M entries from 330,000+ importers (Orrick, Mar 2026).
- Refunds run through CBP's CAPE portal in ACE. Importers of record or their authorized customs brokers file; a declaration holds up to 9,999 entries. Electronic refunds are mandatory (CBP-derived summaries; Mondaq, C.H. Robinson, Sept 17, 2026 advisory).
- Phase 1 covers unliquidated entries and those in the 90-day reliquidation window. Phase 3, for finally liquidated entries, was scheduled Oct 6, 2026. Reports differ on whether it is plaintiff-importers only. I could not confirm Phase 3 actually launched or its final rules.
- Brokers/attorneys are not required for self-filing, but non-Phase-1 entries may need protests or CIT suits, which means attorney involvement.
- Implication (HYPOTHESIS): an unlicensed consultant can prepare data and checklists. Filing on a client's behalf with CBP is customs business and likely needs a licensed broker or the importer's own filing (customs broker rules were not researched). Selling prep as a flat fee avoids contingency-on-government-refund issues but verify with counsel. Check CBP's CSMS and the CIT docket before selling.
- Competition is intense and the window is closing, so this is a short-lived, perishable opportunity.

## 3. Deeper evidence on the top 3

### A. First-party AR follow-up
- FDCPA covers consumer debt only; no federal law governs commercial third-party collection (ABC-Amega, Resolve Pay, via search). States vary: some license commercial collectors and require bonds, with different triggers (creditor location vs debtor location). California and New York details conflicted across sources; California SB 1286 reportedly extends protections to some commercial debts (unverified).
- Safe structure (HYPOTHESIS): operate as the client's agent, using the client's name, domain and email, with no assignment of debt and no paid-on-collection term. Consult counsel and check Wyoming and client-state rules, because "first party" exemptions are state-specific and not verified here.
- Pricing anchor: $1,600/mo AR assistant retainers; agencies take 20-40% or 25-50% (vendor blogs). Small clients are underserved by agencies that need minimum account volumes.
- Finding prospects: HYPOTHESIS only. Overdue-invoice pain shows up with trades, staffing and agencies. No evidence gathered on channels.
- Customer complaints about incumbents: weak evidence. A vendor page argues percentage models cost more; treat as marketing.

### B. Credentialing and payer enrollment
- Pricing is the best-documented here: $100-$300/payer; $2,500-$5,000/provider (or $1,500-$3,500); monthly maintenance $100-$500/provider; $399-$529/provider/mo plans (dental). Sources note delay costs more than the fee.
- Time to cash is good because the work is flat-fee and deposit-friendly, but delivery takes payer processing time of months (ESTIMATE).
- Compliance: BAAs are mandatory when you touch PHI (VERIFIED general rule). Enrollment mostly involves provider identity and licensure data, not patient PHI, but expect practices to ask for a BAA anyway. No HIPAA-compliance cost data was found.
- Gap: small solo practices wanting a fixed price and status updates. Many vendors compete, so differentiation is thin.

### C. Tariff refund prep (see section 2)
- Strong urgency and high dollar amounts, but regulatory and licensing exposure and rapid commoditization by brokers and software.

## 4. Attractive but blocked or slow
- **Public adjusting / commercial claim negotiation**: licensed in about 45 states plus DC; unlicensed activity is criminal in some (Louisiana felony, up to two years) (Insurance Journal, Claims Journal, via search). Blocked. Documentation-only support is the safe edge; exact boundaries are state-specific.
- **Medical billing and prior auth at scale**: BAA, HIPAA security program, breach liability. Not blocked, but 6-10+ weeks to cash and heavy compliance for a solo with $0-$1,000.
- **Contingency audits (freight, telecom, merchant, sales tax, unclaimed property)**: usually 60-180 days to cash (ESTIMATE); fails the 30-day goal unless sold flat-fee.
- **Unclaimed property**: fee caps as low as $1,000 or 10% (DE), PI licenses in NC and IL, waiting periods. Poor economics.
- **ERC**: IRS has closed all non-examined claims as of Dec 31, 2025; about 17,300 open in Aug 2026. Representation needs a credential, and the market is tainted by "ERC mills." Weak fit.
- **Collections as third party**: licensing and bonds in some states; avoid.
