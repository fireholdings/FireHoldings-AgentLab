## (2) Top 3 in depth

### A. New-authority trucking compliance (strongest fit)
- **Pricing:** see row 1 (VERIFIED, vendor pages, 2026). MC authority filing is $300 and starts a 21-day protest period; one source notes charging for the free USDOT number is questionable.
- **Complaints:** one SEO page claims "thousands of negative reviews" about an aggressive compliance vendor; a Truckers Report forum commenter argues it is not a scam. Both unverified. Another forum poster asked "at what cost?" about outside help. Vendors criticize competitors for per-service add-on billing (marketing claim).
- **Prospect lists:** FMCSA Company Census File on the DOT open data portal (dataset az4n-8mr2), filtered by add date; FMCSA Open Data Program page. Third-party Apify scrapers repackage it. Extracts lag; verify on SAFER (VERIFIED via search, otrucking.com guide).
- **Why pay in two weeks:** the audit generally happens within 12 months, with 18 months monitoring (FMCSA brochure). FMCSA reports about 91-92% new-entrant pass rate; vendors claim 15-20% fail first time (marketing). Fear of authority loss and insurance cost is the lever (HYPOTHESIS that it closes in two weeks).
- **Kill risks:** low ticket ($30-$250/mo) with heavy competition; unlicensed-advice risk; FMCSA's new Motus registration system and anti-fraud changes (2026) could alter the new-carrier flow; high carrier churn; tiny customers cannot support a $5M exit without volume.

### B. Credentialing / payer enrollment for small practices
- **Pricing:** row 3. Pain: 20-30 staff hours per provider initial, 10-15 for recredentialing (Medwave, vendor).
- **Complaints:** not found in this research (gap).
- **Prospect lists:** NPPES NPI registry is public (my background knowledge, not verified this session); state medical board license lists (ESTIMATE). New-license and new-practice signals are natural triggers.
- **Why pay in two weeks:** a provider cannot bill payers until enrolled, so delay is lost revenue (ESTIMATE).
- **Kill risks:** payer delays make delivery slow; HIPAA liability; the buyer is a practice manager with existing billing vendors.

### C. Small gov-contractor bid support
- **Pricing:** row 4. Hybrid retainer $3-5K/mo plus 2-5% of awards (vendor blog; federal success-fee restrictions).
- **Complaints:** none found; sources warn quotes are not comparable by scope.
- **Prospect lists:** SAM.gov entity registrations and opportunity listings (public; not fetched this session).
- **Why pay in two weeks:** a live solicitation deadline forces a decision.
- **Kill risks:** lumpy revenue, win-rate dependence, recurring revenue weaker than A/B; no independent data on what a new solo provider can charge.

## (3) Patterns
1. The visible fast cases are services sold by direct outreach or to the founder's existing network (Fame's first client was a former employer; HyperProspect used LinkedIn outreach).
2. Near-zero cost means no equipment, vehicles, or insurance: knowledge work with deadlines. Physical-route niches (rows 8-10) fail the under-$1,000 test.
3. Regulatory or deadline-driven pain beats discretionary marketing spend (ESTIMATE).
4. Price points that appear repeatedly sit in the $100-$500/mo band for tiny customers or $1,000-$4,000 one-time for assessments; reaching $1,000 in 30 days needs about 4-10 small customers (arithmetic).
5. Crowded creative/lead-gen niches show the fastest claimed cash but the weakest moat and exit.
6. Recurring revenue usually comes after a one-time paid diagnostic is converted to a monthly retainer (HYPOTHESIS, consistent with the coaching and fractional-BD examples).
7. No source gave conversion rates; I did not invent any. Next step: allow the two blocked hosts and read primary case studies.
