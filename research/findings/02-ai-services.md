## 2. Deeper evidence on the three best (fast cash + durability)

Ranking is ESTIMATE/judgment, not proven.

### A. Niche-vertical done-for-you back-office with human accountability (#5/#12, tied to #4)
- Why: The acquirer market is active. Frontenac/Gosling (closed 2026-03-23, advised by Baird), iQor/OP360 (Jan 2026), Lean Solutions/SupportZebra (2026-08-24) show PE and strategics buying tech-enabled BPO. One industry commentary says buyers favor BPO platforms with automated workflows and recurring revenue (pcecompanies.com / privsource, VERIFIED as claim). A Beri analysis notes Yellow.ai's SPAC plan to buy and automate outsourcers, with $30M of committed capital against a $205M headline (opinion, undated).
- Caveat: Those are large deals; I found NO evidence of a $5M+ exit for a solo-founder BPO. $5M+ plausibly needs ~$1M+ recurring revenue and a team (ESTIMATE).
- Cash path: sell a fixed monthly fee for one repetitive task (e.g., processing inbound forms) for a niche where you understand the compliance. Do it by hand with LLM help first. Price must be set by interviews; I have no verified benchmark.
- Risk: data confidentiality and error liability; clients may later license a vendor tool that does it.

### B. Voice-receptionist installation for trades/professional offices, sold as a missed-call recovery service (#1/#3)
- Fastest cash: buyers feel the pain directly; the tool is ~$30-$200/mo and you charge for setup and results.
- Weakness: tool pricing is racing to the floor ($29.99 flat at Beside vs $149+ elsewhere), and platform vendors run their own reseller programs (Retell, Trillet, Vendasta, Frontdesk AI). Vendor blogs themselves list churn causes: clients don't see value, get caller complaints, or forget the AI exists (Trillet, undated). Hearth's article on contractor failure modes exists but I did not read its specifics.
- Durability requires: reporting (booked jobs per month), integrations with the scheduler, and a recurring fee tied to outcomes. Exit is likelier as a regional book of business than a $5M company (ESTIMATE); no deal evidence found.
- Legal: Federal recording consent is one-party; several sources claim ~11-12 all-party states but their lists conflict (CT, PA, MI, NV, OR). Needs attorney check. Outbound AI calls fall under TCPA artificial-voice rules.

### C. AI proposal/RFP (and compliance-document) production for small regulated contractors (#9/#10)
- Evidence: Dedicated RFP software exists at ~$830-$899/mo (competitor-written) and enterprise tools are quote-only, which implies SMBs are underserved by software but may be willing to pay for a flat per-proposal service (HYPOTHESIS). I found no actual per-proposal service prices or win-rate data.
- Durability: capture strategy, past-performance narrative, and compliance review are not what ChatGPT replaces; drafting is.
- Your background (program management in regulated settings) fits; however, user said not to bias toward defense, so target commercial RFPs too (MSPs, healthcare vendors, construction subs).
- Exit: GovCon/proposal-services consultancies are plausible acquirers (HYPOTHESIS); no deals found.

## 3. Blunt section: AI agency ideas to avoid

1. **Generic "AI automation agency" (n8n/Make workflows for anyone).** Founder post-mortems: a firm that hit $80K/month with 15 retainer clients shut down after realizing a team member was faster with Cursor than delegating (growwstacks.com blog, undated, via search snippet; self-reported, vendor-adjacent). Another shut a $24K/mo agency and reported a better month afterward (arapovk-site.pages.dev, undated, self-reported). A YouTube-style post cites thin margins. Vendor blogs claim ~70% of churn in first 90 days and ROI not demonstrated as the top reason (ciela.ai, 2025-2026; unsourced survey, treat lightly). Reddit anecdote: $500 retainers invited haggling. Verdict: low barrier, no moat, key-person dependent, nobody buys it at $5M.
2. **"AI for everything" audits as the main business.** The deliverable is increasingly something a client can produce with ChatGPT. Fine as a lead-in, not a company.
3. **Reselling a cheap white-label voice bot with no vertical specialty.** The platform sets the price floor and can go direct.
4. **Outbound AI cold-calling/appointment-setting.** TCPA artificial-voice exposure (FCC 2024 ruling per vendor blogs) and consent liability fall on you or your client. Highest legal downside.
5. **Content/SEO/social "AI" agencies.** Not researched here; I saw only a Digiday story ("confessions: how an indie agency's over-reliance on AI drove it out of business", undated snippet). HYPOTHESIS: commoditized.
6. **Prompt packs / courses / "AI training" for the general SMB.** Free vendor training and YouTube compete.
